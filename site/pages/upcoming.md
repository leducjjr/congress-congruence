# Upcoming changes

<p class="lede">What we're working on next. Dates depend on when each source publishes its data, so
they're estimates. Have an idea? <a href="contact.html#feedback">Tell us</a>.</p>

*Last updated November 11, 2026.*

## In progress {#in-progress}

### 2026 state ballot measures {#ballot-2026}

The measures voters decided on November 3, 2026, compared with how their senators (and the
representative of a state with one House seat) voted on related bills: citizenship requirements for
voting in Alaska, Arkansas, Kansas, South Dakota and West Virginia; voter ID in Arizona, California,
Colorado, Nevada, North Carolina, Ohio and Oklahoma; and Colorado's measure on police cooperation with
immigration authorities. Each state's result is added once that state certifies it, usually within a
few weeks of the election.

## Coming in 2027 {#2027}

### The new Congress {#new-congress}

Members elected in 2026 take office on January 3, 2027. Their pages, seats and votes appear as the
official records publish them, and members who leave move to the former members lists with their
full records kept.

### The 2026 Cooperative Election Study {#ces-2026}

The largest survey on this site publishes its 2026 data the year after each election, usually in the
spring. Its questions about bills will be matched to votes and added when it's released.

## Being considered {#considered}

### Voting records back to 1973 {#records-1973}

The votes of the 93rd to 100th Congresses (1973 to 1988), from Voteview. That would cover the whole
career of every member serving today, and Congress.gov's subject areas for bills also start in 1973.

### Voting trends over time {#trends}

Charts of how things have changed from one Congress to the next: a member's attendance and how often
they voted with their party, and in each chamber, how often votes split along party lines. These would
be counts from the voting records, like every figure on the site, not estimates or scores.

### More surveys {#more-surveys}

We're looking at other large surveys that ask about specific bills and are big enough to estimate
opinion in each state or district. A source is added only if its publisher allows its results to be
republished, and only its questions about proposals Congress actually voted on are used.

## Recently added {#recent}

Newest first. Follow these and every correction in a feed reader: [updates feed (RSS)](feed.xml).

**November 11, 2026**

- **[Members by the numbers](numbers.html):** every member who voted in a Congress, in one table you can
  sort: time in Congress (members who left and came back are counted for the time they served), time in
  their seat, how often they missed votes, and how often they voted Yea, Nay or with their party. Any
  Congress since 1989, House or Senate.
- **Voting records back to 1989.** Every House and Senate vote from 1989 through 2006, about 16,000
  more votes. Longer-serving members' pages now show their records from 1989, with attendance and
  party-line voting for each Congress. Survey comparisons still start in 2006, when surveys large
  enough to estimate each state's and district's opinion begin. See [Sources](sources.html#house).
- **The 2006 Cooperative Election Study,** the survey's first year: what about 36,000 people said on
  partial-birth abortion, stem cell research, an Iraq withdrawal plan, citizenship for illegal
  immigrants, the minimum wage, capital gains taxes and CAFTA, compared with the House and Senate
  votes on them from 2003 to 2006.
- **A 2008 survey question on a constitutional amendment banning same-sex marriage,** compared with
  the House and Senate votes on it in 2006.
- **How long each member has held each seat,** and their time in Congress in all.

**November 10, 2026**

- **Senate votes from the Senate's own files.** Senate votes now come from the official senate.gov
  record, checked daily, instead of a copy that could be weeks behind. Earlier 2026 votes are being
  caught up over the next week or so. See [Sources](sources.html#senate).
- **Choose how many recent votes to see** on a member's page: 50, 100, 250 or 500, with the dates they
  cover.
- **A page for every member and every state,** with addresses you can share, such as
  [All members](members/) and [All states](states/). Shared links show a preview of the figures.
- **[Press and researchers](press.html)** and a **[classroom guide](classroom.html)** for teachers.
- **Nationscape, 2019-2021:** nearly half a million interviews from the Democracy Fund Voter Study
  Group and UCLA, compared with House and Senate votes on background checks, the minimum wage, the
  Dream Act, marijuana, DC statehood, both impeachments and more. Shown as its own source.
- **Hide party labels:** a switch on every page that removes party names and party figures, so members
  can be judged on their votes alone.
- **Compare two members** side by side.
- **State pages:** each state's members and what its residents told surveys.
- **All votes,** with filters, and every member's position on each vote.
- **FAQ** and a **corrections log**.

Every change that corrects a figure on the site is listed in the [corrections log](corrections.html).
