Members by the numbers

Every member of Congress who voted in the Congress you choose, in one table: how long they've served, how often they missed votes, and how often they voted Yea, Nay or with their party. Sort by any column. Every figure is a count from the official records; open a member to see each vote behind it.

    How to read these numbers

    • Time in Congress adds up every stretch a member actually served, in either chamber, from the official member list, so it includes service before the votes on this site begin (1989). A member who left and came back gets the time they served, not the years in between, and is marked "returned". For a past Congress, it's counted up to the day that Congress ended, so "Longest in Congress" means longest at the time.
    • In this seat since is when their unbroken stretch in the seat they held that Congress began.
    • Didn't vote is the share of the chamber's roll-call votes, while they were a member that Congress, where they cast no vote. Members miss votes for many reasons: illness, family, a campaign, or votes held on the day they were sworn in or left. It isn't a measure of effort.
    • Yea and Nay are shares of all their roll-call votes that Congress. They mostly reflect what came up for a vote and who was in the majority, not how agreeable a member is.
    • With their party counts votes where most of their party (or the party they caucus with) voted one way, and how often they voted the same way. Votes where the party split evenly aren't counted.
    • A member who served in part of a Congress is compared on the votes while they were there. One who served in both chambers that Congress appears under each.
