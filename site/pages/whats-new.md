## Recently added {#recent}

**November 18, 2026**

- **How this site was built.** A new page tells the story of how one volunteer and an AI model built
  the site in 12 days: who did what, the technology in plain language, what it costs, and how to do
  something similar. See [How this site was built](how-built.html).

**November 17, 2026**

- **What's new has its own page.** This list used to sit at the bottom of
  [Upcoming changes](upcoming.html), which is now only about what's planned. Both are linked from the
  bottom of every page.
- **Install it as an app.** Add the site to your phone's home screen or your computer and it opens full
  screen with its own icon, and pages you've already viewed open without a signal. Free, with no app
  store, no account and no tracking. A **Get the app** button at the top of every page shows how (or,
  in Chrome and Edge, installs it directly), and it disappears once you're using the app. See
  [Install the app](install.html), which has screenshots of each step.
- **No outside companies.** The typeface now comes from this site's own server instead of Google Fonts,
  so viewing a page no longer contacts any other company. See [Privacy](privacy.html#third-parties).

**November 16, 2026**

- **More state polls on Iran, Venezuela and election rules.** Thirty-five more questions from the
  University of New Hampshire's 2026 polls, now including Connecticut and Rhode Island, and one more from
  the Texas Politics Project. Every question about the Iran conflict is shown beside the Senate's June 23,
  2026 war powers vote, the one that passed, and its note says how many times the Senate voted on Iran
  war powers that year. See [Sources](sources.html#unh-survey-center).
- **One card for a question asked in several states.** When a pollster asks the same question in
  separate state polls the same week, [Surveyed votes](surveyed.html) now shows it once, with each state's
  result on its own line. Each state is still its own poll, and nothing is combined.
- **Filters you can click.** Rows of buttons now narrow long lists: by source on
  [Surveyed votes](surveyed.html) and on member pages; by members, survey questions or votes in
  [search](search.html); by senators, representatives, former members or surveys on state pages; and on
  [Compare](compare.html), by where two members split or agreed on surveyed votes.
- **Faster search.** Searches and All votes now return in a fraction of the time, and vote titles
  that were typed in capitals read normally in search results too.
- **The historical record on the Features page**, with a screenshot of the 1st Congress, and a link from
  the top of [Members by the numbers](numbers.html) to its "How to read these numbers" notes.

**November 15, 2026**

- **UNH Survey Center polls.** Sixteen questions from the University of New Hampshire's polls of New
  Hampshire, Maine, Vermont and Massachusetts adults (June 2025 to January 2026), compared with how those
  states' senators, and Vermont's representative, voted: the One Big Beautiful Bill Act and its 10-year
  ban on state AI rules, the deal that ended the 2025 shutdown, extending the health insurance premium
  subsidies, and U.S. military action toward Venezuela. See [Sources](sources.html#unh-survey-center).
- **Who was asked.** Every comparison now says whether a source asked adults, registered voters, or is a
  count of votes cast. See [Methods](methods.html#who-was-asked).
- **Citizens among residents.** Member and state pages and [Members by the numbers](numbers.html) now show
  how many of an area's residents are U.S. citizens and citizens 18 and over, from the Census Bureau.

- **Texas Politics Project polls.** Three questions from the University of Texas at Austin's polls of
  registered Texas voters, compared with how Texas's two senators voted: approval of the One Big
  Beautiful Bill Act (August 2025), compared with the Senate's vote to pass it, and two questions on the
  conflict with Iran (June and August 2026), shown beside the Senate's June 2026 war powers vote as
  related votes. See [Sources](sources.html#texas-politics-project).

**November 13, 2026**

- **The historical record, 1789 to 1972.** Every House and Senate roll call from the 1st Congress to the
  92nd, about 61,000 votes, in a [separate section](history/) with the same look: a page for each Congress
  with every member's figures and every vote, and a page for each member. Kept apart because the record
  differs, with the parties of the time, changing seats and no surveys; each page says how.

**November 12, 2026**

- **Voting records back to 1973.** Every House and Senate vote of the 93rd to 100th Congresses (1973 to
  1988), about 16,500 more, from Voteview. Every member serving today now has their whole voting record
  here, and [Members by the numbers](numbers.html) and [All votes](votes.html) reach back to January 1973.
- **Who runs this site.** The [About page](about.html#who) now names the site's builder and maintainer.

**November 11, 2026**

- **[Members by the numbers](numbers.html):** every member who voted in a Congress, in one table you can
  sort: time in Congress (members who left and came back are counted for the time they served), time in
  their seat, how often they missed votes, and how often they voted Yea, Nay or with their party. Any
  Congress since 1989, House or Senate.
- **Voting records back to 1989.** Every House and Senate vote from 1989 through 2006, about 16,000
  more votes. Longer-serving members' pages now show their records from 1989, with attendance and
  party-line voting for each Congress. Survey comparisons still start in 2006, when surveys large
  enough to estimate each state's and district's opinion begin. See [Sources](sources.html#house).
- **The 2006 Cooperative Election Study,** the survey's first year: what about 36,000 people said on
  partial-birth abortion, stem cell research, an Iraq withdrawal plan, citizenship for illegal
  immigrants, the minimum wage, capital gains taxes and CAFTA, compared with the House and Senate
  votes on them from 2003 to 2006.
- **A 2008 survey question on a constitutional amendment banning same-sex marriage,** compared with
  the House and Senate votes on it in 2006.
- **How long each member has held each seat,** and their time in Congress in all.

**November 10, 2026**

- **Senate votes from the Senate's own files.** Senate votes now come from the official senate.gov
  record, checked daily, instead of a copy that could be weeks behind. Earlier 2026 votes are being
  caught up over the next week or so. See [Sources](sources.html#senate).
- **Choose how many recent votes to see** on a member's page: 50, 100, 250 or 500, with the dates they
  cover.
- **A page for every member and every state,** with addresses you can share, such as
  [All members](members/) and [All states](states/). Shared links show a preview of the figures.
- **[Press and researchers](press.html)** and a **[classroom guide](classroom.html)** for teachers.
- **Nationscape, 2019-2021:** nearly half a million interviews from the Democracy Fund Voter Study
  Group and UCLA, compared with House and Senate votes on background checks, the minimum wage, the
  Dream Act, marijuana, DC statehood, both impeachments and more. Shown as its own source.
- **Hide party labels:** a switch on every page that removes party names and party figures, so members
  can be judged on their votes alone.
- **Compare two members** side by side.
- **State pages:** each state's members and what its residents told surveys.
- **All votes,** with filters, and every member's position on each vote.
- **FAQ** and a **corrections log**.

Every change that corrects a figure on the site is listed in the [corrections log](corrections.html).
